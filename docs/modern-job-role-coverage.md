## Source and scope

- Prompting article: Ian Miell, [A Family Tree of Software Job Titles](https://zwischenzugs.com/2026/11/03/a-family-tree-of-software-job-titles/).
- [Interactive taxonomy](https://ianmiell.github.io/sw-job-title-family-tree/).
- Inspected both the [Graphviz title source](https://github.com/ianmiell/sw-job-title-family-tree/blob/a13f65be8081de66b02132266f8f1eb2605be069/job_title_family_tree.dot) and [description JSON](https://github.com/ianmiell/sw-job-title-family-tree/blob/a13f65be8081de66b02132266f8f1eb2605be069/descriptions.json) at revision `a13f65be8081de66b02132266f8f1eb2605be069`.
- All **104** title entries are accounted for below: **101** current roles, specialisms or aliases are taught; **3** historical-only entries are explicitly outside scope.
- Older labels still encountered in workplaces, including computer operator, systems programmer, webmaster, analyst/programmer and Hadoop engineer, are included by their current responsibilities. Age is not the exclusion criterion.
- Dates, arrows, lineage claims, popularity predictions and descriptions from the taxonomy are not reproduced. The author describes the chronology and relationships as estimates. The course explanations are original and acknowledge variable employer scope.
- Labels such as prompt engineer and agent/context engineer are presented as emerging and inconsistent. Skills can belong to several jobs; no prediction is made about which title will persist.

## Source discipline and review

Primary sources support the important distinctions: Google SRE and DORA for
reliability/delivery, CNCF for platform engineering, FinOps Foundation for value
and cost responsibilities, the Scrum Guide for accountabilities, dbt Labs for
analytics engineering, Google Cloud for MLOps, Anthropic for context engineering,
Microsoft for AI red teaming, and Palantir for one employer's FDE specification.
These sources illustrate practices and employer scope rather than establishing
universal job-title definitions. Bibliography keys are cited in the textbook.

The review checked distinct service owner/product/project/change/release
accountabilities, technical versus people-management progression, SRE/DevOps/platform
success criteria, data/AI handoffs, shared quality/security responsibility, and
pre-sales/implementation/support/DevRel boundaries. Unsourced salary bands and a
single mandatory entry route were removed from the revised career explanation.

The new narration is authored two-speaker text. Audio regeneration and hosted
publication are separate pipeline operations; source validation or a local render
does not establish that either has happened.


The changed lesson opts in to `require-current-audio`. Its recorded
`audio-source.json` must match the narration filenames/content, audio bytes and
subtitle bytes before media is published or advertised. A successful audio build
records provenance after output validation; `--skip-audio` cannot certify a new
script. Publication binds the transcoded MP3 to the same script, and the existing
audio sync carries that record and subtitles. Until then the lesson uses the
existing transcript and “Audio pending” fallback.

Both A4 and 6x9 PDFs were rebuilt from source. This content update does not
constitute a Kindle/KDP release: EPUB, covers, previous release audit reports and
the external release gates belong to the separately validated release process.
