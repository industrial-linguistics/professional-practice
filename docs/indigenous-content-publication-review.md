# Indigenous Content Publication Review

Status: **external review required before publication**

Scope: Part 7, especially the sections “Balancing Openness and Cultural
Safety” and “Māori Data Sovereignty: A Te Hiku Media Case Study”, plus the
Part 7 framing in the preface and final service-design exercise.

This packet exists because source checking and editorial restraint do not
substitute for review by people with relevant specialist expertise or
community authority. The current manuscript is a release candidate for that
review, not approved publication copy.

## What Has Already Been Changed

- The chapter now distinguishes open-source licensing from Indigenous data
  sovereignty instead of presenting them as equivalent traditions.
- CARE, OCAP®, Te Mana Raraunga, Maiam nayri Wingara, Local Contexts and
  UNDRIP are identified by source and context.
- OCAP® is described as specifically First Nations, and the required FNIGC
  trademark statement appears in the copyright page.
- Detailed claims about Te Hiku Media's internal cloud architecture,
  contracts, staffing, governance forums, training corpus and performance
  were removed where the available public sources did not support them.
- The Te Hiku section now separates sourced facts from professional
  implications and directs readers back to Te Hiku's own material.
- The final service-design exercise warns readers not to select an Indigenous
  organisation as a portfolio subject without the relevant authority and
  leadership.

## Reviewer Profile

At least one reviewer should have current professional or scholarly expertise
in Indigenous data sovereignty. Māori-specific content should also be reviewed
by a Māori reviewer with relevant data-governance, language-technology or
community-authority experience. Australian references should be checked by an
Aboriginal or Torres Strait Islander data-sovereignty specialist rather than
treated as covered by the Māori review.

The reviewer is not being asked to endorse the book, represent all Indigenous
peoples or accept responsibility for the author's final decisions.

## Review Questions

1. Are the named frameworks attributed to the correct peoples and
   organisations?
2. Does the comparison with open source clarify useful differences without
   collapsing Indigenous rights into copyright or licensing?
3. Does any sentence imply that a framework, checklist, consultation or
   training course grants authority to an external practitioner?
4. Are Māori terms used accurately and only where the Māori context supports
   them?
5. Does the Te Hiku section stay within what Te Hiku's public material
   establishes?
6. Are technical controls clearly presented as implementation options
   subordinate to community decisions?
7. Does the fictional archive scenario reproduce an extractive framing or
   suggest a preferred outcome that only the community can decide?
8. Are any important distinctions, harms, obligations or sources missing?
9. Should any material be removed rather than repaired?
10. Is the practice exercise safe for readers to attempt without creating
    unsolicited work for an Indigenous organisation?
