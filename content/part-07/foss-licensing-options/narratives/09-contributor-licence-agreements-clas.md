Speaker 1: A contributor licence agreement sets out what the project may do with the code you contribute: relicense it, defend it in court, or offer it under a second commercial licence.

Speaker 2: There are individual and corporate versions, and the corporate one matters. Check that whoever signs actually has authority to bind their employer.

Speaker 1: Otherwise you learn during an acquisition that a chunk of the codebase has unclear provenance.

Speaker 2: Many projects pair the CLA with a Developer Certificate of Origin, where contributors sign off each commit. That gives you an audit trail per change rather than one signature at the start.
