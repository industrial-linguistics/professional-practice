Speaker 1: Begin with the goal: broad reuse, reciprocal sharing, or open and commercial terms. MIT and Apache 2.0 permit proprietary reuse with notices; Apache also grants patent rights.

Speaker 2: GPL source duties depend on the covered work and distribution. Apache 2.0 code may enter a GPLv3 project, but the reverse is not automatic. Check exact versions and combinations.

Speaker 1: MySQL and Qt illustrate dual licensing, which needs rights to offer both terms and clear contribution agreements. MongoDB's SSPL illustrates a source-available route; it is not OSI-approved open source.

Speaker 2: A government records team might consider GPLv3 for distributed improvements, then review procurement and supplier expectations. Bring legal counsel, community leads, product managers and security engineers together, with an OSPO coordinating. Record compatibility, patent, international and data-sovereignty questions; revisit the choice as the project grows. Like a roommate agreement, clarify obligations before they cause disputes.
