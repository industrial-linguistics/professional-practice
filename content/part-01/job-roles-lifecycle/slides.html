Job Roles Across the Service Lifecycle

Who does what from request to retirement

Design and transition

  • Service owner is accountable for service outcomes
  • Change manager coordinates risk and authorisation
  • Business analysts capture user needs

Operation and support

  • Service desk handles day-to-day issues
  • Application and infrastructure teams troubleshoot
  • Vendors or engineers assist for complex fixes

Continual improvement

  • Problem manager analyses trends
  • Service improvement manager drives changes
  • Ops leadership reviews performance

Career pathways

  • Entry paths include support, development, testing and data
  • Move into specialist or management roles
  • Compare responsibilities, mentoring and decision scope

Read the responsibilities

  • Ask what the person produces and who uses it.
  • Check decision authority, production ownership and on-call expectations.
  • A title can combine several roles; the same title can describe different work.
  • Use the job description to resolve the ambiguity.

Development specialisms

  • Software developer/engineer: design, build, test and maintain software.
  • Front-end: browser interaction and accessibility. Back-end: APIs, rules and data access.
  • Full-stack: work across those boundaries; check the expected depth.
  • Mobile, embedded and game development add platform and domain constraints.

Technical scope and management

  • Senior: own substantial work and help colleagues.
  • Staff, principal, distinguished/fellow: broader technical scope; ladders vary.
  • Technical lead: coordinate technical decisions. Engineering manager: people and team conditions.
  • VP Engineering, CTO, CIO and IT manager: check the portfolio and authority.

Product, delivery and architecture

  • Product manager: priorities and outcomes. Project manager: delivery coordination.
  • Business/systems analysts: needs, processes and system changes.
  • Software, solutions, enterprise and cloud architects: design at different scales.
  • Scrum Master/Agile coach: team effectiveness and improvement.

Infrastructure and delivery

  • Sysadmin, Unix/Linux or Windows administrator: access, patching, backup and recovery.
  • Systems/infrastructure, virtualisation, cloud and Kubernetes engineers: build and operate environments.
  • Build/release and CI/CD engineers: repeatable builds and deployments.
  • Release manager: coordinate scope and timing. Configuration manager: trusted version and relationship records.

DevOps, SRE and platform engineering

  • DevOps: shared delivery and operations practices; the engineer title often means automation.
  • SRE: reliability through engineering, SLOs, error budgets and less toil.
  • Platform engineering: supported self-service capabilities for developers.
  • Overlapping work, different success criteria. Part 3 puts delivery and reliability into practice.

Improving the operating environment

  • Observability engineer: useful logs, metrics and traces.
  • Chaos engineer: controlled resilience experiments with stop conditions.
  • FinOps engineer/analyst: usage, cost allocation and business value.
  • Developer experience engineer and platform product manager: developer friction, needs and priorities.

Networks and security responsibilities

  • Telecoms and network specialists: communications, routing and connectivity.
  • Network administrator, engineer, architect and cloud network engineer have different scopes.
  • Security analyst: investigate. Security engineer: implement controls.
  • Information security officer/CISO: risk, policy and security programme leadership.

Security through delivery and AI

  • Application security engineer: threat modelling and software weaknesses.
  • DevSecOps engineer: security checks and controls in delivery.
  • Penetration tester: authorised testing of agreed targets.
  • AI red teamer: probe AI security failures and harmful behaviour within an agreed scope.

Data responsibilities

  • DBA: database operation and recovery. Data/ETL engineer: reliable data flows.
  • Warehouse/BI developer: analytical stores and reports. Big data/Hadoop engineer: distributed processing.
  • Analytics engineer: tested, documented data models. Data analyst: business questions and results.
  • Statistician/data scientist: study design, uncertainty and statistical or predictive approaches.

AI and machine learning responsibilities

  • ML engineer: production models. MLOps engineer: training, versioning, deployment and monitoring.
  • AI engineer: model-based applications, retrieval, tools and evaluation.
  • Prompt engineer: instructions and examples. Agent/context engineer: state, retrieval and tool use.
  • Titles are still settling. Agree permissions, evaluation, human approval and recovery.

Quality and test responsibilities

  • Software tester: investigate behaviour and unexpected paths.
  • QA engineer: improve quality practices throughout delivery.
  • Test automation engineer: repeatable checks. SDET: test frameworks, harnesses and testability.
  • Ask which risks the checks cover and which still need human investigation.

Support and technical sales

  • Service/help desk: intake and user help. Application support: workflows and data.
  • Support engineer: debugging and reproductions. Customer/field engineer: installation and integration.
  • Sales/solutions engineer or pre-sales systems engineer: discovery, demos and technical fit.
  • L1/L2/L3 are support tiers. Part 5 explains commercial handoffs.

Working with customers and developers

  • Forward deployed engineer: build working solutions close to a customer's problem.
  • IT consultant: advise or implement within an agreed engagement.
  • Technical writer: usable documentation. Developer advocate/DevRel: education and developer feedback.
  • Technology evangelist: awareness and adoption. Agree who maintains customer-specific work.

  • Assign needs, interface, trusted data, unsafe-request testing, release approval and incident response.
